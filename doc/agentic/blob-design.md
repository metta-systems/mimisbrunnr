# Blob storage and content paths — design

Scope: blob writes and reads in `crates/engine`, `crates/transform` (hashing, compression, sector padding, pipeline), chunk cutting and deduplication, `mimir project export`.
Governing spec: SPECIFICATION-DESIGN§9 (pipeline ordering, compression, chunk boundaries), JOURNAL-DESIGN§1.3.1 (chunk write sequence), METADATA-DESIGN§1.2–1.3 (content locator, content map), INDEX-DESIGN§4.3 (chunk index and chunk boundaries).

Plan: [blob-implementation-plan.md](blob-implementation-plan.md) · Open decisions: [blob-decisions.md](blob-decisions.md) · Completed: [blob-completed.md](blob-completed.md) · Order: [operations-plan.md](operations-plan.md)

## Goal

Object content lives in data and packed buckets (ALLOCATION-DESIGN§1.2), located through the object record's content locator (METADATA-DESIGN§1.2), written and read through the transform pipeline. Every object's content is one or more hashed chunks of any size up to the caps below, each replicated, cut and deduplicated per storage policy.

## Selected design

- **Chunks.** Content is a list of immutable, hashed chunks in the content map (METADATA-DESIGN§1.3), every one in the same 88-byte `ChunkEntry` with its own replica set, so the `Replicate` rule applies to every chunk alike. Every chunk verifies against its hash, so verification, fetch-by-hash and resumable transfer work alike for every object.
- **Deduplication is a relation, on by default.** Two entries are deduplicated when they name the same replicas. On write, a chunk whose hash `ChunkIndex` already holds (INDEX-DESIGN§4.3, hash → one live holder) is not stored again: its entry copies the holder's replicas and the object gets its own backpointer to them (METADATA-DESIGN§2.2, one backpointer per owner). Reads never consult `ChunkIndex`. The storage policy turns deduplication off per object or per write (SPECIFICATION-DESIGN§3.5); streams write without it, and background deduplication at idle priority (RECONCILE-DESIGN§1.10) folds their chunks in later.
- **Write:** an object that is one chunk is written with `WriteBlob`: allocate a blob bucket, append the transformed bytes, journal the real extent and its `extent_sum`, set the record's `Extent` locator and its backpointer. Any larger object is written as chunks (JOURNAL-DESIGN§1.3.1) and ends with a `Chunks` locator. A plain chunk is at most one bucket of its disk; a compressed one holds at most `encoded_extent_max` plaintext bytes (default 128 KiB, POOL-DESIGN§1.2), so the cutter ends compressed chunks at that size and random reads decode at most one such chunk.
- **Boundaries** are the chunking axis of the storage policy: chunks as written (`None`), fixed-size, or FastCDC (INDEX-DESIGN§4.3). Since a chunk is rewritten whole, objects taking small random writes need small chunks. Data and journal set share one write barrier; recovery verifies the extents of the unflushed tail (JOURNAL-DESIGN§1.5).
- **Tiny objects** (≤ `INLINE_WRITE_MAX`, 2 KiB by default) are written inside the journal (`WriteInline`) and later packed, many per block, into small-object buckets, so a small file costs neither a separate data write nor a whole 4 KiB sector (JOURNAL-DESIGN§1.8).
- **Streams** are objects in the `Writing` state appended through a per-stream write point in whole buckets, made durable step by step by `AppendChunks` without deduplication, and closed with their final length and incrementally computed content hash; an interrupted stream keeps its durable prefix readable and can be resumed or closed later (JOURNAL-DESIGN§1.8).
- **Blob space comes only from the bucket allocator under copy-on-write commit.** Allocator state, locations and the root pointer commit atomically, so freed blob space is never reused while a committed location still points into it. There is no interim append-only or replay-repaired allocation path.
- **Read:** `DiskEngine::read_blob` follows the record's locator — extent, content-map chunks or packed slot — reads the bytes from a replica, verifies each chunk's hash and inverts the pipeline.
- **Chunk writes** follow the JOURNAL-DESIGN§1.3.1 sequence (`AppendChunks`, `ReplaceChunks`, `TruncateChunks`, `FinalizeObject`).
- **Tweaks** (ENCRYPTION-DESIGN§1): a chunk written with deduplication is encrypted with its hash tweak (`hash[0..8] || block_within_chunk`) and has `CHUNK_FLAG_HASH_TWEAK` set, so equal chunks under one key have equal ciphertext and can share replicas; a chunk written without it (a stream, a `deduplication: off` write) is encrypted with its owner tweak and the flag clear, until background deduplication rewrites it. Packed blocks are tweaked by their block id. Moves never re-encrypt.
- **Equality is visible only through metadata.** Under one key, equal deduplicated chunks have equal ciphertext and name the same replicas. Metadata is encrypted at rest (ENCRYPTION-DESIGN§1, tree nodes under XTS), so someone holding only the disk sees neither hashes nor which objects name a location; with metadata sealed, what remains is that the data area holds fewer distinct ciphertexts than the logical content. Anyone holding the metadata key sees which objects share chunks, as they could compare their hashes anyway. Deduplication does not cross keys: its domain is one encryption key (ACCESS-D7, per-key dedup domains).
- The boundary and deduplication decisions are made at write time from the resolved storage policy ([ontology-design.md](ontology-design.md)).
- Exact-content deduplication uses the BLAKE3 content hash; near-duplicate deduplication is out of scope.
